# Ship the measured core, then land every bag

The bulletproof bot for tonight is not god25 as built. It is v94 plus the rules that were measured to win, with three always-on changes on top: an endgame bank fix, a model that counts the enemy Marcella's stun, and farming through contact on every group we are working. A fresh local check shows that god25 as built loses to v94: 79 of 172 games (45.9%), against 105 of 172 (61.0%) for `_v94all`, which runs the same measured rules without god25's five extra ports. On the same 172 games the paired flips are 28 against 54 (net −26), so those five unmeasured ports (cap rank, pace, bank-hold, flip memory, bush flee) have to come out before anything else. Top-30 games are decided by middle-game (r301-800) farming and deaths, and v95's losses still look like v88's on both: 10.25 deaths a game against the winners' 6.44, and hit% under contact a third of theirs. v95 also loses 6 of 85 games while holding a bag that would have won, or with nothing banked. The user's 70% head-to-head bar against v94 is almost certainly out of reach tonight. The best measured build sits at 61%, the three changes are worth a few points at most, and a 70% local score would still not predict the ladder (local-to-ladder slope 0.24). So the ladder paired SPRT against v94's own results decides, and below the bar v94 stays selected. "Beat every top team by Nov 18" is not a realistic target: we have won 23 of 270 games against the top 30, and reaching rank 30 already needs about +200 Elo, roughly 75% against the opponents we now beat half the time.

## What decides games, and why we sit at 1980

Across 999 decoded top-30-vs-top-30 games, the side with more kills wins **86%** of the time, the side with more r301-800 captures **83%**, more banks 84%, more contested respawn cycles 81%, and more items at r600 78%. Captures by r300 are a much weaker predictor (68%), and altar locks predict nothing (51%). Most games are settled in r301-1000 (563 of 999), and **26%** are won by a team that once trailed by 100+ gems ([counters §3](</home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/research_notes/Bulletproof final bot plan/top30_counters.md>)). Two habits separate the top 5 from ranks 20-30 and hold in their losses too. They hold a recall rather than fight when contacted (ρ +0.53 / −0.49), and they bank often. Kills a game (ρ +0.65), focus fire (+0.58) and never fighting alone (−0.48) track rating on the micro side ([macro](</home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/research_notes/Bulletproof final bot plan/top30_macro.md>), [micro](</home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/research_notes/Bulletproof final bot plan/top30_micro.md>)).

We are a true 1980 bot, not an unlucky one. Over 2,215 ranked games our performance rating is 1978, and we won 1,094 games against an Elo expectation of 1,096. Teams that "own" us explain only 17-21 of the ~200 points to rank 30 ([god24_plan §0, N](/home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/docs/plans/god24_plan.md)). The gap is a lower economic floor plus fights. In like-with-like games against our own opponents (v84-v88), we walk 61% of alive time against their 53%, and we hit camps in 3.8% of contact time against 7.5%. We make 20.7 tributes a game against 30.2, and we earn about a third less gold early (216 against 325 per 100 rounds) ([macro](</home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/research_notes/Bulletproof final bot plan/top30_macro.md>)). v95 repaired the mechanisms it targeted: HoR's dash on retreat rose from 12% to 68%, and Marcella's capture share from 19% to 31%. Its losses still look like v88's. Deaths are 10.25 a game against the winners' 6.44. Walk-ins are 6.6 against 2.4, and their Marcella kills 3.5 of ours. Hit% with an enemy within 6 is 2.7-4.0 against 8-9, and 1v1 contacts at hp ≥ 0.8 are abandoned 40% of the time. The first gem capture slipped to r102, against r68 for the opponents ([issues ledger Q3](</home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/research_notes/Bulletproof final bot plan/issues_ledger.md>), [v95 summary](/home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/experiments/deep_research_nov3/v95/out/summary.txt)). On the ladder, v95 is level with v88 (101 against 102 of 195 paired games) and with v94 (net −4 of 190) ([paired.txt](/home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/experiments/deep_research_nov3/v95/out/paired.txt)).

Local tests mispredicted the ladder for three measured reasons. First, local games against near-copies of ourselves are mostly chaos: 46% of results flip whenever any code changes. Second, a local leader is inflated by about a third through selection. Third, local opponents do not contest or stack the way ladder opponents do (camp hits under contact 3.3% against 9.7%). Over 8 versions, the regression slope of ladder effect on local effect is **0.24 (95% CI −0.65 to +1.14)**, and v95's local +133 gems became −1 on the paired ladder ([test_method](</home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/research_notes/Bulletproof final bot plan/test_method.md>)). Chess-engine testers report the same bias: self-play "over estimates the rating gain" ([talkchess](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=762630)). Tonight's god25 result adds a fourth lesson. Five rules ported in one step without individual measurement cost 15 points of head-to-head against v94. The alternative cause is a mistake in god25's switch-to-constant conversion, and the inert-equal check in change 1 tells the two apart. Deaths rose from 5.9 to 7.6 a game and the margin fell from +108 to −5 ([god25 rows](/home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/experiments/ab2/god25_vs_gpta94/rows.jsonl), [_v94all rows](/home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/experiments/ab2/_v94all_vs_gpta94/rows.jsonl), [loop_log 13:35Z](/home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/experiments/ladder_nov2/loop_log.md)).

## One strategy: contest the middle, die cheap, land every bag

Funnel rewards to the carry, Marcella. Bank so that no bag is ever held when the game ends. This is a pared-down version of what the top 5 do. It does not copy any one team's roster, because rosters differ across the top 5 while these habits do not ([macro](</home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/research_notes/Bulletproof final bot plan/top30_macro.md>)). ## A 70% local bar, a ladder SPRT, and a clock

The local gate runs the candidate against `bots/gpta94` (v94) on `maps/s4` (172 games, both sides) and on the held-out pool (206 games): `/home/claude/analysis/run_vs.sh gpta94 CAND` and `run_vs_pool.sh heldout gpta94 CAND`. The user's acceptance bar is **≥ 265 of 378 games (70%) pooled, with each pool ≥ 65%, 0 faults, fuel p99 < 90k, and deaths not above v94's**. At 70% the standard error on 378 games is about 2.4 points, so a pass is real for these maps. Because the leader of several tries is inflated by about a third, at most two candidates are tested tonight under a pre-set rule. Candidate A is changes 1+2. Candidate B is changes 1-4, and B is preferred only if its s4 score is at least A's ([test_method](</home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/research_notes/Bulletproof final bot plan/test_method.md>)). The ladder then decides with a paired GSPRT against v94's column on the 77 groups (385 games) of `paired_c9.json` (39) and `paired_c10.json` (38). The hypotheses are H0 = 0 and H1 = +4 points, with α 0.05 and β 0.10, giving LLR bounds −2.25 and +2.89. The statistic is LLR = n·0.04·(m − 0.02)/s², and a one-sided sign test at p < 0.05 is the alternative pass, the same rule as the stopping practice of Fishtest-style SPRT ([chessprogramming SPRT](https://chessprogramming.org/Sequential_Probability_Ratio_Test)). The ranked-slot rule holds throughout. Ranked matches are created at every UTC minute where minute % 10 == 0, the champion (v94) is live at every slot, and the candidate is live only in the gap after a slot. Paired groups are queued while the candidate is live, and the 12:50 HTTP-503 incident shows why the re-activation must start at least 4 minutes before the next slot ([loop_log](/home/claude/cambridge_battlecode/experiments/ladder_nov2/loop_log.md)).

## Below 70% tonight, v94 stays selected

The decision rule at 16:08 is set in advance. If the candidate clears 70% locally and passes the GSPRT or the sign test, it is the accepted final bot: live and selected. If it clears 70% but the ladder is undecided, select it only if the net is ≥ 0 over ≥ 385 games; test_method puts the risk of shipping a −3-point version at 9-13%. If it scores below 70% locally, it is not accepted under the user's hard bar. v94 stays live and selected (it is the qualifier entry at 23:59Z), and the candidate's ladder test continues tomorrow as the Nov 4 base. If the user overrides the bar, the only defensible override is a 60-70% candidate with a ladder net ≥ 0 over ≥ 385 paired games. Below 60%, or at a ladder net at or below the fail bound, nothing new ships at all. Keeping v94 costs little: every version from v85 to v95 sits within ±3 points of the others on the ladder. The realistic outcome tonight is the "below 70%" branch. The measured base is at 61% ±7 on s4. Changes 2-4 are worth a few points, so a true 63-66% would show 70% on 378 games only by luck (well under a 5% chance).

The main risks are known. A 70% local result may transfer weakly (slope 0.24). Unmeasured code can cost 15 points, as god25 just showed. Two CPUs are shared with other agents' runs. Activation can fail: an HTTP 503 put v95 into the 12:50 ranked slot, so the submit must start within 1 minute of a slot and roll back if it lands less than 7 minutes before the next one. Opponents update their bots between paired runs (OneTap went 5/5 against v94, then 0/5 against v95 an hour later). New code can push fuel-limited maps over budget. If the bar is missed, the candidate becomes Nov 4's base: it is v94 plus measured rules plus three small fixes, and it is the right starting point for the bisect and the walk-in trace.

## Conclusion

The data changes the question from "which new idea wins" to "which code was measured". The one rule bundle that clearly beats v94 locally (61%) is the measured one. The five rules ported on top of it without measurement cut it to 46%. The cheapest certain gain left is not a fight rule but a bug: bags that are never landed at the end. The strategic gap to the top 30 is real but narrow in kind. They farm through contact, die less in the middle game and bank to 1000 sooner, and the top-30 data says that is what decides 83-86% of their games.

The 70% head-to-head bar is a worthwhile ambition for Nov 18 only if each step toward it is measured on its own and confirmed on the ladder. That means stacking several +2-to-5-point fixes, each of which is a single, traceable change. Tonight the achievable result is a cleaner, measured base that is at least as strong as v94 on the ladder, not a 70% bot.
